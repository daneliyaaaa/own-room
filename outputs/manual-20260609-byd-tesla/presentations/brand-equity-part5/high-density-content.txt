SLIDE 1 — STRATEGIC DIAGNOSIS
Claim: Brand-equity asymmetry requires opposite growth logics.
Tesla diagnosis: premium imagery and resonance are strong; ownership experience, portfolio breadth, and coverage constrain scale.
Tesla strategic job: extend premium equity into more users, occasions, and revenue streams.
BYD diagnosis: salience, performance, and scale are strong; architecture fragmentation and value-for-money associations constrain premium.
BYD strategic job: concentrate meaning, build intelligence cues, and globalize brand equity.
Decision rule: growth must close the weakest CBBE layer without damaging the strongest one.

SLIDE 2 — ANSOFF PORTFOLIO
Market penetration:
- Tesla: service response, transparent repair, owner community.
- BYD: clarify Dynasty/Ocean roles; reduce internal overlap.
Market development:
- Tesla: lower-tier cities and accessible segment with protected tech experience.
- BYD: localized overseas communication, retail, service, and trust.
Product development:
- Tesla: compact model, MPV/utility formats, software bundles.
- BYD: intelligent-driving, cockpit, OTA, premium design consistency.
Selective diversification:
- Tesla: license selected FSD/platform capabilities.
- BYD: connect mobility, battery, storage, and energy solutions.
Priority logic: penetration first, adjacent development second, diversification only with brand fit.

SLIDE 3 — TESLA EXECUTION BLUEPRINT
Objective: broaden access without becoming ordinary.
1 Ownership flywheel:
Action: service-level standards, transparent repair progress, proactive customer care.
Brand mechanism: converts performance satisfaction into trust, attachment, and advocacy.
KPIs: service NPS, repair cycle time, complaint closure, referral rate.
Risk/control: service investment dilutes margin -> prioritize high-friction touchpoints and digital triage.
2 Selective access:
Action: accessible model/trim and lower-tier-city footprint.
Brand mechanism: expands salience while retaining technology leadership.
KPIs: new-to-brand buyers, consideration in target cities, premium perception.
Risk/control: price cuts erode prestige -> cost-down by platform efficiency, not de-contenting.
3 Use-case expansion:
Action: compact vehicle, MPV/utility formats, software/service bundles.
Brand mechanism: expands purchase occasions and lifetime value.
KPIs: portfolio concentration, software attach rate, repeat purchase.
Risk/control: complexity weakens simplicity -> shared platform and strict design language.

SLIDE 4 — BYD EXECUTION BLUEPRINT
Objective: convert industrial strength into coherent premium and global brand equity.
Short term, 1-2 years:
Action: clarify Dynasty/Ocean roles; define Denza, Fangchengbao, and Yangwang ladders; strengthen visible intelligence cues.
Brand mechanism: reduces confusion and upgrades imagery/judgment.
KPIs: cross-model confusion, premium consideration, intelligent-tech association.
Medium term, 3-5 years:
Action: move from product export to localized brand building, retail, service, and cultural relevance.
Brand mechanism: builds credibility, feelings, and resonance outside China.
KPIs: overseas awareness, consideration, service satisfaction, price realization.
Long term, 5+ years:
Action: evolve corporate meaning from NEV maker to green-technology company across mobility, batteries, and storage.
Brand mechanism: creates a broader, future-facing master-brand purpose.
KPIs: corporate brand value, non-auto association, ecosystem adoption.
Architecture guardrail:
Use existing premium marques for upward movement; no overlapping new sub-brand.

SLIDE 5 — BREAKTHROUGH BETS, RISKS, AND GOVERNANCE
Tesla breakthrough:
License selected FSD capabilities to become an intelligent-mobility platform and standard setter.
Upside: ecosystem reach, recurring revenue, category authority.
Risks: safety liability, regulation, loss of exclusivity.
Gate: license modular capabilities only after safety and brand-control thresholds.
BYD breakthrough:
Make Yangwang/Fangchengbao intelligent-premium flagships rather than create another marque.
Upside: premium proof, clearer technology halo, faster brand upgrading.
Risks: cannibalization, architecture overlap, fragmented investment.
Gate: exclusive audience, price band, design code, and channel role for each marque.
Governance:
Quarterly brand-equity dashboard covering premium perception, consideration, NPS, price realization, and cross-brand confusion.
Final rule: approve growth only when it strengthens core meaning and one weak CBBE layer.
